This journal started with a collage theme because collage works the way multimodal design does, with its separate pieces, each in its own mode, arranged until together they make meaning. For my final entry, I wanted to hand that arranging over to the reader, as opposed to summarising eight entries in one more block of text.

Each note below holds one idea I took from an earlier entry. Drag or click a note onto the board and it becomes part of one final image. You can place them in any order.

## Looking back, and looking ahead
When this module began, I saw instructional design as a rigid process. Feeling quite out of my depth, I prioritised learning the theories, applying the frameworks, and then hoped the designing would follow. That view isn't entirely wrong, because the theory is what makes a design defensible. However, I experienced a shift in Practical Project 2. Basing my whole project on a conceptual platform and a pedagogical AI agent felt like a push at first. But the facilitator's experience was so nuanced that I decided to get creative with it, and the ideas kept growing. Then empathy changed my thinking further. When I put myself in the facilitator's shoes, I saw that the large volume of ideas for each unit would add up to an overbearing, overstimulating programme that they would learn very little from. Making the design realistically achievable was not a loss of creativity. It was creativity guided by empathy, and it echoes what my overloaded five-outcome draft taught me in Entry 5.

If one idea holds this collage up, I think it is evolution. Iteration is part of it, but evolution describes how something can start in one place and end somewhere quite different and unexpected. You look at it from new angles and add pieces you never planned to add. My PP2 design evolved like that, and so did this journal. I expect that any instructional design work I do in the future will evolve in the same way.

I also accept that no designer can be sure how much choice is too much, or that a decision was the right one, because learning is unique to every learner and every group. That is why UDL makes the biggest difference, and why I think the honest response to that uncertainty is to test early, collect feedback from real users, and stay open to endless iterations.

Looking ahead, I have two plans. The first is practical and more immediate. I don't want a lack of technical skill to restrain my ideas, so I hope to build one unit of CoTrace in Moodle or Articulate Rise to see what the design looks like on a real learning management system and, of course, also to become more familiar with the mainstream platforms. The second, more long-term, goal is to eventually test CoTrace with facilitators or educators before any students. They are the experts within education, so their feedback should show me what I haven’t thought of, since my design still rests on theory. It is a cascade, with feedback moving up and down the line.

What I want to carry forward is the balance. I want to solve problems intellectually, with the theory built in, and also creatively and empathetically, as an artist would. Above all, I don't want to hold myself back from being innovative. Like a collage, I am WRITE SOMETHING BETTER HERE.
