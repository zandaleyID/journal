## Looking back and looking ahead
When this module began, I saw instructional design as a rigid process. Feeling quite out of my depth, I prioritised learning the theories, applying the frameworks, assuming the designing would follow naturally. That view isn't entirely wrong, because the theory is what makes a design defensible. Letting myself get creative with the facilitator’s experience produced many ideas, and putting myself in their shoes showed me that they would add up to an overwhelming programme. Making the design realistically achievable was not a loss of creativity. It was creativity guided by empathy.

If one idea holds this collage up, I think it is evolution. Iteration is part of it, but evolution describes how something can start in one place and end somewhere quite different and unexpected. You look at it from new angles and add pieces you never planned to add. My PP2 design evolved like that, and so did this journal. I expect that any instructional design work I do in the future will evolve in the same way.

I also accept that no designer can be sure how much choice is too much, or that a decision was the right one, because learning is unique to every learner and every group. That is why UDL makes the biggest difference, and why I think the appropriate response to that uncertainty is to test early, collect feedback from real users, and stay open to endless iterations.

Looking ahead, I have two plans. The first is practical and more immediate. I don't want a lack of technical skill to restrain my ideas, so I hope to build one unit of CoTrace in Moodle or Articulate Rise to see what the design looks like on a real learning management system and, of course, also to become more familiar with the mainstream platforms. The second, more long-term, goal is to eventually test CoTrace with facilitators or educators before any students. They are the experts within education, so their feedback should show me what I haven’t thought of, since my design still rests on theory. It is a cascade, with feedback moving up and down the line.

What I want to carry forward is this balance. I want to approach problems intellectually, with theory guiding my decisions, while also thinking creatively and empathetically, as an artist would. Although my niche is the ethical and beneficial integration of AI into learning, I want to make use of the possibilities AI tools offer without losing sight of the human creativity and empathy at the heart of good design. Above all, I don't want to limit my own innovation. Like the collage, I am still taking shape, with each new experience adding something to the bigger picture.
