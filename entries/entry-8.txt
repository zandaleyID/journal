This entry looks at how I built the storyboard for PP2, the tool I used, and something the tool showed me about my own design. Instead of a storyboard, I created a detailed wireframe, took screenshots of it, labelled the transitions between slides and added a few annotations. I did this to make the learning path as explicit as possible, including what facilitators do, how they progress and interact with the course, not just what the course contains. I over-annotated at first and had to condense it so the path stayed clear, which also made it much easier to write about afterwards.

The authoring tool I used to create the wireframe was Whimsical. It doesn’t offer a foundational walkthrough, so I needed to look for tutorials and guidance at the start, but once I knew where things were, it became easier to use. The endless frames mattered most to me. I could design several options in one place and move them around instead of working one page per frame, and that was an affordance (Day and Lloyd, 2007) I actually used.

However, something I only noticed while reflecting is that I had to learn Whimsical independently, and I had built the Unit 1 walkthrough for a similar reason – so that facilitators could learn the platform at a practical level before moving on to the pedagogy. I made this the foundational unit because it made sense to me, and the Whimsical experience only showed me why afterwards.

It also made me see that how a course is structured should be communicated at the start, so that learners know what to expect from their own path. Biggs and Tang (2007) describe goals made clear upfront and advance organisers that give learners a framework to fit new material into. Wang et al. (2013) report students who were confused and frustrated when they weren’t sure what was expected of them, and Laubscher and Bosch (2020, citing Hammarlund et al., 2015) note that communicating information early supports motivation and planning. I would now treat making the learning path clear from the start as a deliberate design decision.

[question]

reply: Thank you for sharing. Knowing the path early can take a lot of weight off, and noticing when you didn’t have it is part of designing well.
