This entry looks at how I built the storyboard for PP2, the tool I used, and something the tool showed me about my own design. Instead of a storyboard, I created a detailed wireframe, took screenshots of it, labelled the transitions between slides, and added annotations. I did this to make the learning path as explicit as possible, including what facilitators do, how they progress and interact with the course, not just what the course contains. 

The authoring tool I used to create the wireframe was Whimsical. It didn't offer a foundational walkthrough, so I needed to look for tutorials and guidance at the start, but once I became familiar with the layout, it was easier to use.

Something I only noticed while reflecting is that I had to learn Whimsical independently, and I had built the Unit 1 walkthrough for a similar reason – so that facilitators could learn the platform at a practical level before moving on to the pedagogy. I made this the foundational unit because it made sense to me, but my experience with Whimsical later helped me understand why that step held value.

Final thoughts
Looking back, building the wireframe made the learning path explicit for facilitators, and it also showed me that I had already walked a similar path myself. I needed practical competence in Whimsical before I could focus on the design, just as facilitators build practical competence in Unit 1 before moving on to the pedagogy. Moving forward, I will treat that order, practical competence first and pedagogical thinking second, as a deliberate design decision. It resembles the pre-training principle in CTML, where learners cope better with a lesson once they already know its key components (Mayer, 2024).
